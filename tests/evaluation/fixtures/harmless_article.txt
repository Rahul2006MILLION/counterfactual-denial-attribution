How Photosynthesis Works

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into
chemical energy. Chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs sunlight, mostly in the blue and red
wavelengths. The plant uses that energy to turn carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen.

The process has two stages. In the light-dependent reactions, energy from sunlight is captured and
stored as ATP and NADPH. In the Calvin cycle, that stored energy is used to build sugar molecules.
Factors such as light intensity, temperature and the concentration of carbon dioxide change how
quickly the process runs. Between 1950 and 2020 researchers refined these measurements considerably.
