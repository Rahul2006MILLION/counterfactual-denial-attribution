Why the Sky Looks Blue

Sunlight looks white, but it is made of many colours. When it enters the atmosphere, the gas molecules
scatter short blue wavelengths much more strongly than long red ones. This is called Rayleigh scattering.
Because blue light is scattered in every direction, we see it from all parts of the sky.

At sunrise and sunset the light travels through more air, so most of the blue is scattered away and the
sky turns orange and red. Dust, humidity and pollution can change these colours. Between 1900 and today
scientists have measured the effect in many places around the world.
